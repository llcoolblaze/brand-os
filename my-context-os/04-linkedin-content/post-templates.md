# LinkedIn Post Templates

> These templates provide repeatable structures for LinkedIn posts. The goal is
> consistency without sameness -- use these as scaffolding, then bring your
> unique voice (see brand-voice.md) and experience to each post.

---

## Hook Formulas

The hook is the first 1-2 lines visible before "...see more." It determines whether
anyone reads the rest. Every post must open with a strong hook.

### Attention Hooks

|------|---------|---------|

## Post Structures

### Structure 1: Story Post

**Best for:** Lessons learned, case studies, personal experiences
**Pillar fit:** Personal, any pillar with a narrative angle

```
[Hook: What happened]

[1-2 sentences of context / setup]

[The turning point / insight]

[2-3 specific takeaways, each on its own line]

[Closing question or CTA]
```

**Example (adapt to your stories):**

```
Last month, we lost our biggest deal of the quarter.

The prospect loved the product. The champion was strong.
The demo went perfectly.

But we made one fatal mistake: we never met the CFO.

At the 11th hour, the CFO killed the deal because
"we didn't understand their financial model."

3 lessons I'll never forget:

1. A great demo means nothing if the economic buyer isn't bought in
2. Multi-threading isn't optional -- it's survival
3. Ask "who else needs to say yes?" on EVERY discovery call

Have you ever lost a deal you thought was locked in?
```

---

### Structure 2: Insight / Framework Post

**Best for:** Thought leadership, frameworks, mental models
**Pillar fit:** Industry expertise, methodology

```
[Hook: Surprising insight or reframe]

[Context: Why most people get this wrong]

[Your framework / model -- use line breaks and formatting]

[Quick example showing the framework in action]

[CTA: Save this / Share this / What would you add?]
```

---

### Structure 3: Contrarian Take

**Best for:** Engagement, differentiation, thought leadership
**Pillar fit:** Industry expertise, positioning

```
[Hook: "[Popular opinion] is wrong."]

[Why everyone believes it]

[What you've seen that contradicts it]

[Your alternative take, with evidence]

[Caveat / nuance to show you're not just being provocative]

[Discussion prompt: "Am I off base? What do you think?"]
```

---

### Structure 4: How-To / Tactical

**Best for:** Value delivery, saves/shares, lead gen
**Pillar fit:** Methodology, tools, processes

```
[Hook: "Here's exactly how I [achieved outcome]:"]

Step 1: [Action]
- [Detail or tip]

Step 2: [Action]
- [Detail or tip]

Step 3: [Action]
- [Detail or tip]

[Results you got from following this process]

[CTA: "Want the full template? Comment 'template' and I'll send it."]
```

---

### Structure 5: Data-Driven Post

**Best for:** Authority building, shareability
**Pillar fit:** Industry insights, research

```
[Hook: Surprising data point]

[Where the data comes from (credibility)]

[2-3 implications of this data]

[What this means for [ICP persona]]

[Question: "Does this match what you're seeing?"]
```

## Character Count Guidelines

| Element | Guideline | Rationale |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **Hook** | < 150 characters (ideally < 100) | Must be visible before "see more" |
| **Total post** | 800-1,300 characters | Sweet spot for engagement |
| **Maximum** | 3,000 characters | LinkedIn's limit; longer posts need exceptional content |
| **Paragraphs** | 1-3 lines each | White space = readability on mobile |
| **Line breaks** | Use generously | Wall of text = scroll past |
| **Emojis** | <!-- TODO: 0 (professional) or 1-3 per post (approachable) --> | Match your brand voice |
| **Hashtags** | 3-5 max, at the end | More than 5 looks spammy |

## Formatting Best Practices

1. **One idea per post.** If you have two ideas, make two posts.
2. **Front-load value.** The first 2 lines determine if anyone reads the rest.
3. **Use line breaks aggressively.** Every sentence or thought gets its own line.
4. **Bold your key insight.** Not every line -- just the one line you want remembered.
5. **End with a question.** Posts with questions get 2-3x more comments.
6. **Write like you talk.** Read it aloud -- if it sounds stiff, rewrite it.
