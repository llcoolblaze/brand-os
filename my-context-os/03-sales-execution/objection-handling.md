# Objection Handling

> Every objection is a buying signal -- it means they're considering you seriously
> enough to voice concerns. This document provides a structured framework for
> handling objections and specific scripts for the most common ones.

---

## Response Framework: LAER

Use this 4-step framework for ALL objections before going to specific scripts.

| Step | What It Means | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **L**isten | Let them finish. Don't interrupt or get defensive. | (Pause, nod, take notes) |
| **A**cknowledge | Validate their concern. Show you understand. | "That's a fair concern. A lot of our customers felt the same way initially." |
| **E**xplore | Ask a clarifying question to understand the real objection. | "Can you help me understand -- is it the total cost, or the timing of the investment?" |
| **R**espond | Address the root cause with evidence. | "What we've seen with similar companies is..." + proof point |

> **Key principle:** The first objection stated is rarely the real objection.
> Always explore before responding.

## Objection Prevention

The best objection handling is prevention. Address common objections before they arise.

| Common Objection | When to Preempt | How to Preempt |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Price | During value demonstration, before pricing reveal | Build ROI case so the price feels like a bargain |
| Competition | During discovery | "Who else are you evaluating?" -- get it on the table early |
| Timing | During discovery | "What's driving the timeline?" -- find the compelling event |
| Authority | During discovery | "Walk me through the decision process" -- map it early |
| Adoption | During demo | Show ease of use, share adoption metrics proactively |
