# Discovery Questions Framework

> Discovery is the most important sales conversation. The goal is NOT to pitch --
> it's to deeply understand the prospect's world so you can determine fit and
> position your solution as the obvious answer. These questions are sequenced to
> build trust and uncover the truth, not interrogate.

---

## Discovery Call Structure

| Phase | Duration | Goal | Tone |
|-------|----------|------|------|
| Opening | 2-3 min | Set agenda, build rapport, earn permission to ask questions | Warm, professional |
| Situation | 5-7 min | Understand their current state | Curious, non-judgmental |
| Pain Discovery | 8-10 min | Uncover the real problems and their root causes | Empathetic, probing |
| Impact Quantification | 5-7 min | Quantify the cost of the problem | Consultative |
| Decision Process | 5-7 min | Map how they buy | Direct, matter-of-fact |
| Next Steps | 3-5 min | Confirm mutual interest and schedule next action | Confident, clear |

**Total:** 30-40 minutes

---

## Opening Questions

**Goal:** Set the context and earn the right to ask deeper questions.

<!-- TODO: Adapt these to your style and ICP -->

1. "Thanks for making time. Before we dive in -- I've done some research on [company],
   but I'd love to hear it from you. Can you give me the 60-second version of what
   [company] does and your role?"

2. "What prompted you to take this meeting? Was there a specific trigger, or more
   general exploration?"
   > *Why this matters:* Their answer reveals urgency level and framing.

3. "I want to make this valuable for you regardless of whether we end up working
   together. What would make this 30 minutes well-spent for you?"

---

## Situation Questions

**Goal:** Understand their current state without making assumptions.

<!-- TODO: Customize to your domain -->

4. "Walk me through how you currently handle [the problem your product solves].
   What does that process look like day-to-day?"

5. "What tools or systems are you using for this today?"
   > *Why this matters:* Reveals status quo / competitive landscape.

6. "How long have you been doing it this way?"

7. "How big is the team that touches this process?"

8. "On a scale of 1-10, how well is the current approach working?"
   > *If they say 7+:* "What would need to change to make it a 10?"
   > *If they say <5:* "What's the biggest gap?"

---

## Pain Discovery Questions

**Goal:** Go beneath surface symptoms to find the root pain. Use layered questioning.

<!-- TODO: Adapt to your specific pain points from ICP personas -->

9. "What's the biggest challenge you're facing with [process/problem area] right now?"

10. "How long has that been a problem?"
    > *Why this matters:* If it's been a problem for years, something changed to make it urgent now.

11. "What have you tried to solve it?"
    > *Why this matters:* Reveals what hasn't worked (position differently) and investment tolerance.

12. "What happened when you tried that?"

13. "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about [area], what would it be?"
    > *Why this matters:* Gets to the dream state -- use their words in your proposal.

14. "Who else on your team is affected by this problem?"
    > *Why this matters:* Identifies multi-threading opportunities and scope of impact.

15. "What happens if you don't solve this in the next [6/12] months?"
    > *Why this matters:* The "cost of inaction" question. If the answer is "nothing much,"
    > there's no urgency and this deal will stall.

---

## Impact Quantification Questions

**Goal:** Put a number on the pain so your price seems small by comparison.

<!-- TODO: Build ROI questions specific to your value prop -->

16. "You mentioned [pain point]. Can you help me quantify that? How much time/money/
    effort does that cost you per [week/month/quarter]?"

17. "How many [deals/customers/projects] are affected?"

18. "If this problem were solved, what would that mean for your [revenue/team/goals]?"

19. "What's the opportunity cost? What could your team be doing instead of [manual process]?"

20. "When your [CEO/board/leadership] asks about this area, what metrics do they look at?"
    > *Why this matters:* Connects your solution to what leadership actually cares about.

### Example Impact Calculation (use during the call):
> "So if I'm hearing you right -- your team spends about [X hours/week] on [process],
> that's roughly [annual cost in salary]. And the error rate means you're missing about
> [Y%] of [opportunities/revenue]. So the total cost of this problem is somewhere around
> [$Z/year]. Does that sound about right?"

---

## Decision Process Questions

**Goal:** Map the buying process so you don't get surprised later.

21. "Have you purchased a solution like this before? What did that process look like?"

22. "Besides yourself, who else would need to be involved in this decision?"

23. "Is there anyone who might be opposed to making a change here? What would their
    concerns be?"
    > *Why this matters:* Identifies blockers early.

24. "What's the approval process? Is there a formal procurement step, or can your team
    decide independently?"

25. "What criteria will you use to evaluate options?"
    > *Pro tip:* If they can't articulate criteria, help them build it -- framed around
    > your strengths.

---

## Timeline Questions

26. "Is there a specific date or event driving this decision?"
    > *Why this matters:* Compelling events create real urgency. No event = deal will drift.

27. "What's your ideal timeline for having a solution in place?"

28. "Are there any internal deadlines I should know about -- budget cycles, board meetings,
    planning cycles?"

---

## Budget Questions

29. "Have you set aside budget for this, or would this need to be a new budget request?"

30. "What range are you expecting for a solution like this?"
    > *If they won't share:* "I'm not trying to anchor pricing -- I want to make sure I
    > don't waste your time with options that aren't in the right ballpark."

31. "Who owns the budget for this area?"

---

## Next Steps Questions

32. "Based on what we've discussed, here's what I'm thinking as a logical next step:
    [specific proposal]. Does that make sense?"

33. "Who should be included in our next conversation?"

34. "Can we get [date/time] on the calendar before we wrap up?"
    > *Why this matters:* Never end a discovery call without the next step booked.
